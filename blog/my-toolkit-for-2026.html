My toolkit for 2026

Another year, another toolkit update. I’ve been doing these annual snapshots since 2019, and this one feels more like the 2023 and 2024 editions than last year’s: an overhaul rather than a few swaps. There are new tools across writing, coding, and personal design work, and much more of my day now runs through AI.

Hardware

Almost everything stayed the same this year, which I’ll take as a good thing. The additions were the AirPods Pro 3 (my sister took my AirPods Pro 2), the Akko Air 01 (replaced the Keychrons, the NuPhy Air75 V3, and the Lofree Flow 2), and the Sony RX100 VII. The camera is small enough that I think it can finally start going everywhere with me instead of sitting in my tech drawer.

On my desk

  • MacBook Pro M4 Pro 14-in (home) and MacBook Pro M3 Pro 16-in (office)
  • Apple Studio Display (home)
  • Rapoo MT760 Mini
  • Akko Air 01
  • Magic Trackpad
  • HomePod mini
  • Bose QuietComfort Ultra

On the go

  • AirPods Pro 3
  • Audio-Technica ATH-CKD3C
  • iPhone 17
  • iPad mini 7 + Apple Pencil
  • Sony RX100 VII

Software

Work & productivity

Dia + Safari — Dia is the default browser on my laptop, Safari on my phone and iPad

Tinycast — a native app launcher with an inline calculator, window management, emoji picker, and Raycast extensions without the bloat

CleanShot X — screenshots, annotation, video recording, OCR. The new Studio Mode is awesome.

Dashlane — password manager

Spark Mail — personal email, the same setup on my laptop and my phone. RIP Notion Mail, which I was using for work email.

Raindrop — bookmarks and links

CliperX — clipboard history app with iCloud sync that doesn’t cost a fortune

Hotspot Shield — VPN that comes with my Dashlane subscription

AI

Superwhisper — the best dictation software out there, in my opinion. I oscillate between dictation and typing by hand depending on what I’m doing.

Claude — prototyping, coding, research, and design

ChatGPT — searching and quick chats, with Codex sometimes handling one-off coding tasks

Design & development

Figma — where a good chunk of my design work still happens. Moving between Figma and Claude via MCP means I can stay in the design loop instead of treating them as separate tools.

Paper — where I play around with personal design ideas for this site. It holds the design tokens and a loose structure for the homepage and blog, so I can quickly mock up something, test a font or layout, or see an idea on a canvas. I also briefly tried pen.dev and was impressed, but I prefer Paper.

Zed — my code editor and the one that finally replaced Sublime Text for me

Ghostty — my default terminal

Writing & organization

iA Writer — my new default for writing long form, including every post here

Apple Journal — daily entries on my phone and laptop

Writer — notes and everything in the archive I want to find again. I haven’t abandoned Obsidian, but I open it far less on my laptop now, even if I still use it on my phone. Writer has what I need in a simpler, more minimal interface.

Travel & finance

Dime — monthly budgeting

Wallet by BudgetBakers — tracks all my accounts

Numi — calculations using natural text

Xe — currency conversion when I need to quickly compare exchange rates

Flighty — flight tracking

Media, reading & photography

Apple Music — music. AutoMix on iOS 27 is really good.

Apple Books — I read all my books here

Audible — audiobooks for when I’m on the road or want a break from reading

Readwise — resurfaces book highlights so I remember what I read. The redesign is fantastic and definitely overdue.

feeeed + Reeder — RSS readers

Photomator — editing my RAW photos

Zerocam — the new PRO mode now lets you control every setting so I’m back to using it

Utilities & system tools

Vorssaint — all-in-one utility app that replaces probably a dozen apps I was using before: BetterDisplay, MiddleClick, CleanMyKeyboard, Mac Mouse Fix, and likely a few more that I forgot

LookAway — reminds me to rest my eyes

Mole — cleans up caches and junk

BatFi — keeps my laptop’s battery healthy

Mojito — type : anywhere to find emoji and GIFs without leaving the keyboard. This habit from Slack finally snuck up on me.

Monocle — nicely blurs everything except the active window when I need to focus

Karabiner-Elements — keeps shortcuts consistent across my keyboards

Dropover — shelf for moving files around

Clop — optimizes images, videos, PDFs, and whatever lands on the clipboard

Folder Preview — Quick Look for folders in Finder

Markdown Preview — should have come in as a built-in app really

Browser extensions

Fontanello — shows a page’s typography from the right-click menu. I love peeking at fonts, and I’ve kept it around for years.

Remove YouTube Shorts — hides Shorts entirely, less brainrot (or that’s the hope)

Obsidian Web Clipper — mostly for the reader mode when I read articles

Decentraleyes — serves common libraries locally instead of from CDNs

Minimal Theme for Twitter / X — strips X back to be more minimal and hides ads

Modern for Hacker News — restyles HN

Modern for Wikipedia — restyles Wikipedia

That’s it for 2026, so far. I still have a couple of months to change my mind, but for now, this is what my desk, my backpack, and my dock look like. The Sony had been out of its drawer, too, by the time I finished writing this post.
